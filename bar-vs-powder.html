Section 03

The Core Problem with Powder

Your friends are right about one thing: GLP-1 users often struggle with solid food, and a liquid format is easier to consume. But that argument collapses when you examine what a powder version of your product would actually look like.

Your bar's differentiation doesn't survive the format change

The Dark Chocolate Tahini Bar has three competitive advantages: (1) micronutrient density from whole foods, (2) a clean 12-ingredient label, and (3) a GLP-1 positioning in an empty category. Here's what happens to each of those in powder format:

Advantage #1 — Whole-food micronutrients: Destroyed. Your bar delivers 33% DV magnesium from pumpkin seeds, cacao, almond butter, and hemp seeds. A powder can't contain these ingredients in scoopable form — you'd need to use magnesium citrate or magnesium oxide instead. The entire nutritional story shifts from "whole foods your body recognizes" to "synthetic vitamins blended into protein isolate." Kate Farms already does this with 27 vitamins and minerals. You would not be differentiated.
Advantage #2 — Clean 12-ingredient label: Destroyed. Your bar's label reads: egg white protein, dates, almond butter, pumpkin seeds, cacao powder, tahini, hemp seeds, chia seeds, collagen peptides, green tea extract, sea salt, vanilla extract. Every ingredient is a recognizable whole food. A powder version would read: pea protein isolate, natural flavors, stevia leaf extract, xanthan gum, magnesium citrate, zinc gluconate... You become Orgain. You become KOS. You become every other powder on the shelf.
Advantage #3 — Uncontested GLP-1 category: Destroyed. In bar format, you have zero direct competitors combining GLP-1 positioning + clean label + micronutrient density. In powder format, you're entering a space where Youtheory already has GLP-1-branded protein powder at retail, Kate Farms has a Danone-backed 25g/160cal/27-vitamin shake at Walmart, and Orgain is the #1 plant protein brand in America at $1.35/serving. Your moat evaporates.

The digestibility argument has a simpler answer

If some GLP-1 users can't tolerate solid food, the solution isn't to abandon the bar format — it's to tell those customers to break the bar into pieces and eat it over 30–60 minutes, or to crumble it into yogurt. This is already standard advice from obesity medicine specialists for GLP-1 patients eating any solid food. A bar that can also be crumbled into a soft format is more versatile than a powder that can only be a shake.

Section 05

Recommendation

Stay with the bar.

The powder format eliminates every competitive advantage that makes this product worth building. The bar occupies an empty category. The powder enters a $27 billion war zone with Danone already on the field.

The strategic logic

The protein powder market is not a white space — it's a red ocean. Youtheory has already claimed the "GLP-1 protein powder" position with a doctor endorsement and retail distribution. Kate Farms, backed by Danone's balance sheet, is delivering 25g protein with 27 vitamins and minerals at Walmart. Orgain owns the clean-label plant-protein powder category at $1.35/serving. A bootstrapped startup entering this market has no defensible position.

The protein bar market, by contrast, has a genuine gap. No product combines GLP-1 positioning, whole-food micronutrient density, clean caffeine, and zero added sugar. Not David (fitness-focused, artificial sweeteners). Not RXBAR High Protein (15g added sugar). Not ALOHA (no GLP-1 positioning, no micronutrient strategy). Your bar is genuinely the first product purpose-built for this consumer.

The powder's advantages — higher protein-per-calorie, easier digestibility, longer shelf life — are real but non-decisive. They don't create a differentiated product. They create a commodity entering a market where incumbents have 10x your resources.

What about a powder later?

Phase 2 consideration, not Phase 1. Once the bar establishes the brand in the GLP-1 space and builds a customer base, a companion powder or shake could make sense as a line extension — similar to how Youtheory launched protein alongside a multivitamin and probiotic. But the bar must come first because it's where you have an uncontested position. You don't launch your second product before your first one has proven demand.

Bottom line

Your friends are solving the wrong problem. The question isn't "which format is theoretically better for GLP-1 users?" — it's "which format gives a bootstrapped startup with $10K–$20K the highest probability of building a defensible business?" That answer is unambiguously the bar. You have a validated recipe, a proven bootstrap model (RXBAR), an empty competitive category, and a whole-food ingredient story that simply cannot exist in powder form. Ship the bar.
