Home Prototype Recipe

This recipe produces 8 bars for taste testing. The goal is to lock the flavor profile — texture will be refined during co-manufacturer onboarding. Make it exactly as written for Batch 1, then iterate.

Ingredients — Batch of 8 Bars (~480g)

Egg White Protein Powder88g (~6 tbsp)
Medjool Dates, pitted112g (~8 dates)
Almond Butter80g (~5 tbsp)
Pumpkin Seeds (pepitas)64g (~½ cup)
Unsweetened Cacao Powder48g (~½ cup)
Tahini40g (~3 tbsp)
Hemp Seeds (hulled)24g (~3 tbsp)
Chia Seeds16g (~2 tbsp)
Collagen Peptides16g (~1 tbsp)
Sea Salt4g (~¾ tsp)
Vanilla Extract3g (~½ tsp)
Water (if needed)1–3 tbsp
Skip the green tea extract for this taste test. At 80mg per bar, it's a fraction of a gram — completely tasteless. You're testing the chocolate-tahini-seed flavor profile. The extract gets added at manufacturing.

Equipment

Food processor (essential), 8×8" or 9×5" loaf pan, parchment paper, kitchen scale (strongly recommended), spatula, knife.

Section 02

Method

1
Soften the Dates
If dates are dry/firm, soak in warm water 5–10 min, then drain. Fresh soft Medjool dates can skip this. Pit and tear each into 2–3 pieces.
5 min
2
Pulse the Pumpkin Seeds
Pulse 5–8 times until roughly chopped — some pieces, some powder. You want texture variation. Transfer to a large mixing bowl.
1 min
3
Process the Date-Butter Base
Add dates, almond butter, and tahini to food processor. Process 30–60 seconds until thick sticky paste. Scrape sides. Should look like dark chunky nut butter — not perfectly smooth.
2 min
4
Combine Dry Ingredients
To the mixing bowl with pumpkin seeds, add: egg white powder, cacao, collagen, hemp seeds, chia seeds, sea salt. Whisk until evenly combined.
1 min
5
Combine Wet + Dry
Add date-butter paste and vanilla into dry mix. Mix with spatula or hands — takes muscle. Should be thick, dense, slightly sticky. If too dry/crumbly, add water 1 tbsp at a time until cohesive.
3–5 min
6
Press into Pan
Line 8×8" pan with parchment (leave overhang). Press mixture firmly and evenly — compaction prevents crumbling. Aim for ¾" thickness.
2 min
7
Refrigerate
Cover and refrigerate at least 2 hours, ideally overnight. Chia seeds hydrate and create a gel matrix that binds everything. Don't rush this.
2+ hours
8
Cut & Taste
Remove with parchment overhang. Cut into 8 equal bars (~60g each). Wrap individually. Store refrigerated 7–10 days or freeze up to 2 months.
2 min
Texture expectations: Home prototype will be denser/chewier than commercial. Commercial bars use industrial presses, controlled humidity, and precise temperature. Don't evaluate texture critically — you're testing flavor. If taste is right, co-manufacturer dials in texture.
Section 03

Evaluation & Iteration

What to Evaluate

Sweetness
Naturally sweet, not dessert. If too flat, add 1–2 more dates per batch. If too sweet, reduce by 1–2 dates.
Chocolate Intensity
Dark, rich, brownie-like. If too bitter, swap 8g cacao for dates. If you want more, increase cacao 8g.
Tahini Character
Earthy, savory depth. If polarizing, cut to 20g. If you love it, push to 50g. Adjust almond butter inversely.
Salt Level
Biggest lever for "finished" vs flat. You may need 50% more. Salt suppresses bitterness and amplifies perceived sweetness.
Crunch Factor
Pumpkin seeds provide crunch. Less pulsing = bigger pieces. More pulsing = smoother. Adjust to preference.
Protein Powder Taste
Egg white can add slight chalkiness. Cacao and almond butter should mask. Try different brands if gritty.

Iteration Roadmap

Batch 1: Make exactly as written. Taste. Take notes on the six criteria above. Share with 2–3 trusted people for unfiltered feedback.

Batch 2: Adjust based on feedback. Most common first-batch changes: more salt, slightly more dates, tweak tahini-to-almond-butter ratio. Change 1–2 variables at a time.

Batch 3: Lock the flavor. This becomes the reference recipe you hand to a co-manufacturer for scale production with proper food science (water activity control, shelf stability testing).

The gap between home and commercial: A co-manufacturer will fine-tune moisture content, may add a small amount of natural preservative (citric acid or vitamin E/mixed tocopherols) only if needed, and use industrial forming equipment. But the flavor you develop at home is the blueprint they work from. This is exactly how RXBAR started — Peter Rahal made bars in his kitchen before scaling to a co-packer.
